EU Ratings Reveal Reduced Battery Lifespan for Samsung's New Galaxy Foldable Lineup

via GSMArena2026-07-24

New regulatory filings in Europe have revealed a significant trade-off for Samsung's latest foldable lineup, according to GSMArena. Official listings from the European Product Registry for Energy Labelling confirm that the new silicon-carbon batteries inside the Galaxy Z Fold8, Z Fold8 Ultra, and Z Flip have a reduced lifespan compared to previous generations.

While incorporating silicon-carbon technology allowed Samsung to boost the Fold8 Ultra's capacity by 600mAh over the Fold7, it impacts longevity. The new foldables carry an EPREL rating of 1,200 endurance cycles, representing a noticeable drop from the 2,000 endurance cycles officially rated for the older Z Flip7 and Z Fold7 models.

For potential foldable buyers, battery health over time is a critical consideration when investing in an expensive flagship device. While higher energy density provides better short-term battery capacity, a shorter cycle lifespan means heavy users may see degraded battery performance earlier in the device's life cycle than on older Galaxy foldables.

Galaxy Z Fold 8 Silicon-Carbon Batteries Trade Operational Lifespan for Energy Density

via 9to5Google2026-07-24

Samsung has officially adopted silicon-carbon battery technology for its new Galaxy Z Fold 8 series, but the upgrade comes with a notable drawback. According to 9to5Google, while these high-density batteries offer modern power benefits, their overall operational lifespan is reduced compared to conventional lithium-ion batteries used in earlier hardware.

Silicon-carbon battery chemistry allows smartphone manufacturers to fit higher energy capacities into thinner, lightweight device frames. However, the trade-off with this newer technology is faster chemical degradation over time, meaning the internal power cells cannot sustain their original peak charge capacity through as many total recharge cycles.

For buyers planning to hold onto their foldable phone for several years, reduced battery longevity is a vital factor to weigh. Consumers who keep devices long-term may need to budget for earlier battery replacements or accept shorter screen-on times as the silicon-carbon cell ages faster over extended daily use.
